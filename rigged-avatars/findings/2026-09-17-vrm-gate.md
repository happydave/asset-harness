## Goal

with one reusable gate, and make the empty-stub pattern machine-checkable — a shape claimed as authored must

## What it found out about the files

- **A stub has no geometry.** The exported face mesh carries 29 morph targets, not 52, and a stub expression
  has no `morphTargetBinds` key at all. "Displaces by exactly zero" is therefore defined over an
  expression's binds, and a control binds a stub to a genuinely flat target to show the rule is about
  displacement and not about being unbound.
- **The file and Blender agree.** All 29 authored displacement figures read from the exported POSITION
  deltas match the generator's own shape-key measurements within 0.1 mm (`jawOpen` 43.48 mm against 43 mm
  nominal; `browInnerUp` 28.61 mm against 30).
- **VRM 0.x is the half turn the discovery described, now measured:** eyes at z = −107.0 mm and
  `leftEye.x` = −68.0 mm, against +107.0 and +68.0 in the 1.0 file. The gate asserts both frames.
- **The add-on's `Duplicated bone` warnings are not in the files.** They print during the generator's 0.x
  re-import; both exported humanoids have 21 bones on 21 distinct nodes.
- **glTF-Validator:** 0 errors on both files. The 1.0 file draws 10 infos (`UNSUPPORTED_EXTENSION` for
  `VRMC_vrm`, and `UNUSED_OBJECT` on every `TEXCOORD_0` — the avatar has no texture until the toon lane).
  The 0.x file adds one warning, `INVALID_EXTENSION_NAME_FORMAT`, because VRM 0.x's extension is named
  `VRM` with no vendor prefix, and 87 `BUFFER_VIEW_TARGET_MISSING` hints. Listed by the gate, not judged.

## Results

| Input | Rows | Exit |

| Fresh pair from ai2 (1.0 + 0.x) | 253 passed | 0 |

The full run on the pair takes 3.3 s on `ai`; the contract stage alone 0.2 s on the 28 MB 1.0 file.

**Defects seen to fail, each at the row that targets it.** Eleven file edits (`mutate.py`): renamed key,
dropped bind, stub bound to a live target, stub with a 4 mm delta (carried by a sparse accessor), reindexed
binds, a flattened shape, case drift in a clip name, transposed gaze origin, a wrong preset weight, a
falsified accessor `max` (validator only), plus the flat-target control that must *pass* the stub row. Five
through the real exporter on ai2: `old-facing` (faces +Z row, and all 29 frame rows, because the camera is
looking at the back of the head), `flat-morph`, `nonzero-stub` (10.0 mm on `cheekPuff`), `stray-object`
(`Cube` in the mesh-node set), `blender-frame-offset` (gaze origin).

## What looking at the artifact caught

The first contact sheet passed every row and was wrong twice. The camera was fitted to the morph mesh's
bounding box, which takes in the neck and shoulders, so the face filled under half of each frame's height; and the
"neutral frame shows the avatar" row compared against the corner pixel, which the torso covered, so it was
counting the wrong thing and passing. The framing now comes from the eye bones, and the background
reference is the same frame rendered with the avatar hidden.

Two limits of the pixel checks, both measured: zeroing a shape's POSITION deltas while leaving its NORMAL
deltas still changes 5,197 pixels through shading alone, so a pixel diff by itself cannot say a shape moved;
and `nonzero-stub`'s 10 mm bulge sits on the back of the head where the front camera cannot see it. The
buffer measurement catches both. The pixels are the second reading, not the first.

One thing for the art lane: every frame shows a thin dashed line running from the chin down the torso.
