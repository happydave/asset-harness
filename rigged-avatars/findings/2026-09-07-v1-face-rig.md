## The topology: apertures with concentric loops

Each eye and the mouth is an **aperture** rather than a painted region. `inset_region` applied repeatedly
to an elliptical face patch lays down concentric loops; deleting what remains opens the hole and leaves the
loops behind as the lid. A blink is then a lid folding shut over an eyeball — not a dent in a shell.

Measured on the built mesh: **eye aperture 44.6 × 28.8 mm, mouth 56.8 × 20.0 mm**, three loops each, on a
128 × 72 UV-sphere shell scaled to semi-axes 160 × 157 × 182 mm.

**Four things this construction taught, all of them by failing first:**

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| **`use_even_offset` spikes vertices off the model** | The ellipse patch has a staircase border full of acute corners, and even-offset mitring threw one vertex to **x = −1.65 m on a 0.16 m head**. It did not crash — it surfaced as an absurd rim measurement that silently zeroed every eye and mouth morph. Use `use_even_offset=False`, and keep a "no vertex escapes the head volume" check. |
| **`inset_region` returns the rim, not the interior** | Tracking the face list across passes and filtering it against the returned faces walked the boundary in by **one** inset's worth in total instead of three. Re-select the patch against a shrinking ellipse each pass — it is independent of the op's return semantics. |
| **An aperture's lower rim is not a function of x** | Cut from a quad grid it is a staircase: measured, it steps between −4.4 mm and −14.6 mm within a 27 mm span. So "close the upper lid onto the lower rim" reads a different z depending on which vertex it finds — it stopped 11 mm open on one attempt and drove 10 mm *through* the lower lid on the next. **Converge both lids on one closure line instead**: exact by construction, needs no rim measurement, and is what a real blink does anyway. |
| **A socket cone renders in front of the iris** | A cone from the aperture rim to an apex behind the eye necessarily passes *through* the eyeball. The eyeball is the seal instead: radius 32 mm covers the aperture's furthest corner (26.5 mm out) against any front-on ray, flattened to 0.78 in y and recessed so it does not breach the shell beside the eye. The mouth keeps its cavity cone — nothing sits inside the mouth to intersect. |

Two further gotchas worth keeping: the shell is a **zero-thickness surface with holes in it**, so without
backface culling the camera sees its interior through each aperture as a black jagged wedge (culling also
exports as glTF `doubleSided: false`, which is what a toon shader wants); and a **step function in a morph
mask tears the mesh** — a constant displacement inside a rectangular brow band folded the forehead, and a
hard `|x| > 12 mm` smile cutoff read as a jagged W. Both wanted a falloff.

## The contract (this is what WI 930 inherits)

- **52 ARKit clips declared**, exact camelCase, one morph per custom. **16 authored, 36 empty stubs** —
  the sanctioned VSeeFace pattern.
- **Stub reasons are recorded per shape, not per project**: 8 are stubs because gaze is bone-driven,
  12 have no visual referent on a stylized face, 3 are not webcam-driveable at any authoring effort, and 13
  are deferred to the tranche work item. The realistic archetype revisits the soft-tissue ones — the split
  is a property of the *archetype*.
- **13 of the 18 presets are morph-composed**, with **fractional weights** (a viseme is a partial jaw plus
  a partial mouth shape; the exporter's previous hard-coded 1.0 could not express one). `neutral` is empty
  by definition and the four `look_*` are empty because gaze is bone-driven — that is correct, not a gap.
- **Bone-driven gaze**: `eye.L`/`eye.R` bound to the optional `left_eye`/`right_eye` humanoid slots with
  `look_at.type = 'bone'`. Verified live on v4.4.0 by introspection before it was planned, not from memory.
- **Expression override modes** (`none|block|blend`) on presets only — a raw ARKit custom is the tracker's
  own channel and must not be made to fight it. Blinks block look-at; emotions blend the mouth; `surprised`
  blocks blink.

## The VRM is 6.7× the size of the .glb, and it is the exporter

Measured, from the same mesh and the same four primitives:

| | stored morph elements | file |
|---|---|---|
| `.glb` (glTF exporter) | `POSITION` 12,808 — **sparse**, no normals | 2.5 MB |
| `.vrm` (VRM add-on) | `POSITION` 605,744 and `NORMAL` 605,744 — **dense** | 16.9 MB |

`export_try_sparse_sk=True` is accepted by the VRM operator and has **no effect** on its output. Removing a
material changed the file by 7 KB, so the material split is not the driver — *that hypothesis was tested
and refuted*. The only lever available to us is fewer mesh vertices. This matters for the next two work
items: the tranche roughly doubles the morph count on this mesh, and the toon art lane is where modelled
feature regions would be replaced by a painted texture.

```
~/blender-4.2/blender --background --python blender_v1_face_rig.py -- --out DIR [--previews]
~/blender-4.2/blender --background --python blender_v1_face_rig.py -- --out DIR \
    --negative-control {flat-morph|nonzero-stub|stray-object}

```

Deterministic — parametric geometry, fixed displacements, no sampling. Exit code is non-zero if any check
fails.
