# Clamor — field lore (night session, 2026-07-25)

Grounded in the game's own vocabulary (design-gameplay.md): the colony back home, runs into a
zombie-infested dead city, **noise-driven aggro** (the world is quiet and you keep it that way),
roles **Scout / Medic / Fixer / Scavenger / Soldier**, the **Screamer / Brute / Lurker**, and **Grit** —
the scarce heroic pool, "got this far on nerve alone." These are creative artifacts to seed lyrics and
video; not canon until the owner says so.

---

## The Hold — a working bible

The colony is called **the Hold**. Not a city, not a fort — a stubborn knot of people behind a wall
they rebuilt three times, on the far side of a river from a city that died mid-sentence. The Hold does
not grow food fast enough. So it sends out **bands** — one, two, three at a time — into the quiet.

The city is *quiet*. That is the whole horror of it. The dead don't roam looking for you; they wait,
slack, in doorways and stairwells, until a sound reaches them. A dropped can. A forced lock. A shout.
Then they come, and more come behind them, because the first ones make noise of their own. This is the
**clamor** — the thing the game is named for, the thing every run is a negotiation with. You do not
clear the city. You move through it *without waking it*, take what you can carry, and leave before the
noise you couldn't help making arrives.

A band lives and dies on three things:
- **Silence** — the Scout's whole art. Movement that generates no noise. She opens the map without
  waking it; she is why the others get to be careful instead of lucky.
- **Time** — the round. Danger escalates whether you act or not. Standing still is a decision, and it
  costs.
- **Grit** — what's left when the plan is gone. You get a little, it rarely comes back, and the mission
  turns on the one moment somebody spends it: a Sprint across an open street, a Stabilise at range with
  the horde two tiles out, a door held shut by a Fixer who should have run.

Nobody at the Hold asks a returning band what they saw. They ask what they brought, and count the
faces, and that is the whole liturgy.

## Mini-stories (specific events)

### 1. The quiet mile (a Scout, Silent Move)
Mara went first, the way she always went first — heel to toe, weight rolled forward, the map opening
in front of her like held breath. Silent Move is not invisibility; the dead in the stairwell could
*see* her fine if they looked up. They didn't look up. That was the trade: she gave the band a clean
mile of dead city and asked nothing back but that they match her, step for step, and none of them ever
quite could. By the pharmacy she had counted eleven of them standing in the dark like coats on hooks,
and walked the band around every one, and not a single can dropped. The loud part came later. The quiet
part was hers.

### 2. What Stabilise costs (a Medic, Grit, at range)
Dell had two things left when Okonkwo went down in the open: three action points and one Grit. The
book says you close, you kneel, you raise them. The book assumes the street is empty. Dell spent the
Grit instead — Stabilise at range, a thing you can do maybe twice a mission and never twice in a row —
and Okonkwo's chest hitched and filled from forty feet away while Dell never left cover. He made a
mistake survivable, which is the only thing a Medic is really for. He was out of Grit for the rest of
the run. He knew it when he spent it. He spent it anyway. That is the whole job.

### 3. The one that saw us (a Screamer)
It wasn't a Brute that ended the run at the depot — Brutes you hear coming, Brutes you can plan around.
It was thin and grey and had been a person recently enough to still be wearing a lanyard, and it stood
up in the corner of the Scavenger's vision and *saw them*, and then it did the thing Screamers do: it
told the city. One sound, rising, and every slack shape in three blocks turned its head at once. After
that the run stopped being about supplies and started being about the door — who held it, how long, and
how much Grit the Fixer had left to spend on hinges that were never going to hold anyway. They got out.
Four went in. Ask the Hold and they'll tell you four came back, and leave out how close *four* came to
being a different number.

### 4. Attendance (the colony's side)
There is a woman at the Hold whose only job is the gate log. She writes down who goes out and, when a
band comes back, who came back, and the arithmetic between those two numbers is the entire weather of
the place. Good weeks the columns match. She has learned not to look up when a band comes in short,
because looking up makes them explain, and no one should have to explain the arithmetic on the same day
they lived it. She just writes the smaller number, and lets the Larder cheer for the crates, and keeps
the gate.
